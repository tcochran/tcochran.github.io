Continuing the exploration of what maximizing developer effectiveness means in the AI era. A common question a lot are struggling is what should the product development workflow be in an AI first approach? And tactically how does the software development process change from a day to day point of view for a developer, what are the tools and services they interact with, what are their new jobs to be done. 

When we think about it, there is a trap, where we take the current SDLC, with our current process and tools, and throw AI at it to optimize. This is a reasonable first step, it will work and provide benefits, as the AI agents are very good at mimicking what Humans do. However, it will be efficient and involve a lot of constant supervision and interventions from the developer. It won’t provide that 10X productivity improvement that is promised.  

My original article I started with a low/high effectiveness day in the life, I will attempt to repeat that in for the AI era, based on the most advanced teams and platforms I have worked with and researched. Reflecting the rate of change and innovation at the moment, I fully expect this to generate lots of conflicting opinions and be out of date very soon\!

For the team that I would regard as highly productive, with a high throughput 5-10 PRs a week per developer. There are a lot of problems with this metric which we will explore in a future article. However, a team increasing their PRs dramatically, is interesting. It is a team you should talk to, to understand if their value to customers, and customer satisfaction has increased similarly.

To prepare for an AI Native future ,we start with the premise,that the Agent will do all the heavy lifting of development and operational task. The developer, except on the rare exception, will not do any direct changes. What does that mean for the workflow that the developer and the cross functional team should do through. A good platform team, has adopted a product focused approach, where they are optimzing for the experience of the Customer the developer. With the shift to Agentic workflow, it’s important to keep that customer focus, but not we have two Customers, the developer as the technical SME, the ideator, designer, supervisor etc.. and the Agent performing development and operational tasks. In means revisiting our jobs to be done, not just trying to perform the jobs to be done with a AI applied. For example with there is a lot of focus on code review, as it’s quickly become a bottleneck with AI. We could make a normal process easier with AI, we could provide suggestions, these are tactical solutions. How do we get to the point, that we have higher confidence in the code being reviewed, how can we ensure it’s functional and non-functional completeness, reducing the amount of rework and the time to do review. For their different steps we should do in planning or design that will reduce a review bottleneck.

## Onboarding 

Traditional onboarding. Maybe involve setting up your laptop. Going through an onboarding wiki, to setup your environment. Probably plating wack on whole, with permissions and problems you have (was that wiki up to date). Pairing and asking questions on slack. In a good, after a couple of weeks the environment is up, and the developer has enough understanding to do a basic task. 

When a feature took a week to code and get read for production, onboarding a developer for a couple of weeks, was probably OK. Now in an agentic world that same feature might take half a day. Where does teave onboarding? This is a recurring theme about developer friction, when development was slow there was lot of friction that is overlooked, probably the team knew they can do better, but product deadlines meant it never got poritixedas as the application got larger and more complex it’s harder to fix. 

In an AI Native workflow a developer is typically working with multiple agents, they are either doing a very active pairing and iterative mode, or they are doing a slower feedback, where they send off a precise determination specification that they don’t expect too many issues.
